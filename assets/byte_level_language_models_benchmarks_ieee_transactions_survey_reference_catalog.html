Four architectural families solve the byte sequence-length bottleneck

Raw UTF-8 byte streams expand sequence lengths by roughly 4× compared to BPE subword tokens, causing quadratic self-attention cost to explode in standard Transformers. Between 2016 and 2026, tokenizer-free research converged on four structural remedies: linear-time recurrent/state-space backbones (MambaByte, EvaByte), fixed-size byte patching (MEGABYTE, bGPT), dynamic boundary or entropy-driven patching (BLT, SpaceByte, Bolmo), and end-to-end differentiable hierarchical U-Nets (H-Net, AU-Net, ByteFlow Net).

Critically, literature checks through November 2026 confirm that no standalone peer-reviewed IEEE, ACM, or TACL survey focuses exclusively on byte-level language models. Existing 2023–2026 reviews examine tokenization broadly (Cognetta et al., 2026; Vico & Libovický, 2026) or discrete multimodal vector quantization (arXiv:2507.22920), creating a well-defined publication opening for an IEEE Transactions survey.

Dynamic patching and retrofitting closed the 8B-parameter scaling gap

Early tokenizer-free models either operated at sub-billion scales or incurred severe inference latency. By 2025–2026, entropy-based patching (BLT), multi-stage autoregressive U-Nets (AU-Net), chunked linear attention with multibyte prediction (EvaByte), and subword-to-byte retrofitting (Bolmo) scaled byte-level pretraining and adaptation to 6.5B–8B parameters while matching compute-controlled subword LLMs.

Editorial verification notes and citation disambiguation for IEEE manuscripts

Before pasting entries into an IEEEtran.bst bibliography, account for four publication edge cases uncovered during primary-source verification:

  • ByteFlow disambiguation (two distinct papers): Cite ByteFlow: Language Modeling through Adaptive Byte Compression without a Tokenizer (Deng et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv:2603.03583) for tokenizer-free LLMs. Do not conflate it with the unrelated ACSAC Workshops 2025 networking paper (A Byte-Level LLM for Deep Packet Inspection and Network Intelligence, IEEE CSDL).
  • Bolmo dual title (arXiv vs. Nature): The December 2025 preprint (arXiv:2512.15586) is titled Bolmo: Byteifying the Next Generation of Language Models, whereas the October 2026 Nature publication (Vol. 658, Issue 8137, pp. 915–922, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11111-4) is titled Retrofitting language models to operate over bytes (Cambridge Apollo record).
  • EvaByte citation format: EvaByte was released via the HKU NLP technical blog (Jan 21, 2025) with open GitHub code and Hugging Face weights, rather than a standalone arXiv preprint; cite it as an @misc online technical report per IEEE style.
  • Unpublished weights & moved repositories: MEGABYTE, SpaceByte, Charformer, Hourglass, Perceiver AR, ByteFormer, and ByteFlow Net have no official Hugging Face model weights verified as of November 2026. CharBERT's repository moved from the paper's original footnote path to github.com/mawentao277/CharBERT.
